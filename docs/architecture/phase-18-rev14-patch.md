## 8a. M-17 — origin equality is asserted before arming, for every routed protected request (REPLACES rev 13 7g.3's proposed 6j.1(7) amendment and its residual-difference list; AMENDS rev 13 7g.2 and 7g.4; AMENDS AC-086; NEW AC-096)

> **Superseded and deferred by owner decision OWNER-12 (Option C):** no protected non-GET request reaches this mechanism in Phase 18; the text in the stated extent is preserved unchanged as an input to the deferred item 18P-PROTECTED-NONGET, is not a Phase 18 requirement, and nothing is deleted. **Extent: section 8a only. Sections 8b and 8c remain NORMATIVE (F18 to F23) except as re-scoped by rev 17 section 11d.7.** See rev 16 sections 10a and 10b and rev 17 section 11k.

SEC-13's ruling is adopted in full: **M1 as specified in rev 13 7g.3 is not accepted, and the rev 13 narrowing of rev 12 6j.1(7) is not accepted as written.** The design-only closure SEC-13 described (remediation 1) is specified below. SEC-13's remediation 2 ("Option C") is an owner scope choice and is re-presented, not taken.

### 8a.1 The rule (normative)

1. **Definitions.** At arming time, for a protected action:
   - `pageOrigin` = the serialized origin of the page's **top-level** document (not the frame's, not the opener's);
   - `approvedOrigin` = the origin recorded in the consumed ledger approval;
   - `targetOrigin` = the serialized origin of the request URL that will be issued at hop 0.
   Each is computed with the WHATWG `URL` parser and compared as the **serialized origin** (scheme, host, port), ASCII-lowercased, with the default port omitted, userinfo rejected, and a trailing dot on the host rejected rather than normalised away. Comparison is `===` on those strings. No host-suffix, no eTLD+1, no "same-site" relaxation: **origin equality, not site equality.**
2. **Gate, before arming.** Falcon arms a route for a protected request **only if `pageOrigin === approvedOrigin === targetOrigin`**. Any inequality, or any origin that is opaque or non-fetchable (`about:`, `blob:`, `data:`, `file:`, `null`), is a refusal **before the route is armed and before any request is issued**: `POLICY_PROTECTED_CROSS_ORIGIN_UNSUPPORTED`, **exit 18**, with the three origins named in the audit entry and in the failure reason. The step fails, the run fails, the page is torn down. This is a prevention claim, and it is sound because it is evaluated from declared and already-known data before Falcon issues anything — the same category as rev 13 7g.2 rule 4, not the category of observation.
3. **Scope: every routed protected request, not only M1.** SEC-13 T7 shows that plain `route.continue()` already suppresses the CORS preflight, so the defect is in the route layer and not in M1. The gate therefore applies to **any** protected request Falcon routes, under **any** R-7 option that arms routed protected requests, and it is independent of which mechanism performs the request.
4. **No cross-origin routed protected request exists in Phase 18.** Normative, and stated as an absolute: under every option that remains available, Falcon never routes, performs, continues, fulfils or emulates a protected request whose target origin differs from the page's top-level origin and the approved origin. A manifest that declares one is a manifest error, refused at load (`POLICY_PROTECTED_CROSS_ORIGIN_UNSUPPORTED`, exit 18) rather than at arming.
5. **Hop-0 re-check, as detection.** Immediately before performing the hop-0 request inside the route handler, the three origins are recomputed and compared again, because the page's top-level origin can change between arming and dispatch. A mismatch aborts the route and fails the run (`POLICY_PROTECTED_ORIGIN_CHANGED`, exit 18). **This is defence in depth and is labelled as detection, not prevention:** whether `route.abort()` reliably prevents transmission of hop 0 itself is **UNVERIFIED** (rev 13 7g.0 established only that abort at hop 0 does not prevent later browser-followed hops). No prevention claim rests on it.
6. **Cross-origin `Location` is never followed and never performed.** If the hop-0 response is a 3xx whose `Location` resolves cross-origin, the response is discarded exactly as for any 3xx (M1's existing behaviour) and **no hop is performed**. Combined with rule 2, no cross-origin transmission of an approved body can occur through Falcon at any hop.

### 8a.2 Rewritten 6j.1(7) narrowing (REPLACES the block quoted in rev 13 7g.3)

> **6j.1(7), rev 14 restatement.** `route.fetch({maxRedirects: 0})` remains **withdrawn** as a means of reconstructing, replaying, emulating or continuing a redirect chain, and its result remains **never** evidence about browser-followed redirect behaviour. It is permitted **solely** as the performer of the single approved hop-0 request of a protected action **whose target origin is equal to both the page's top-level origin and the approved origin** (section 8a.1), where its response is either passed through unchanged (non-3xx) or discarded entirely (3xx), and where no subsequent hop is performed. For any protected request that is not same-origin in that sense, `route.fetch` is **withdrawn without exception** and the request is refused before arming. The limitation text must state that even in the permitted same-origin case the request carries **no `Sec-Fetch-*` headers**, that the page observes **204** rather than the application's response, and that a `Set-Cookie` on a discarded 3xx is not applied to the context.

### 8a.4 R-7 — the three options re-presented with M-17 applied, and **NOT chosen**

R-7 remains an **owner** item. Rev 14 updates each option for section 8a and selects none. Option A's gain claim "no cross-origin body leak in those cells" is **struck** (m-48): the cross-origin Q3 baselines had no landing hop either, so the measurement does not discriminate.

**Option A′ — protected non-GET for subresource `fetch`/`XHR` only, via M1, same-origin only.**
- *Scope:* M1 at hop 0 of a protected non-GET dispatched as a subresource `fetch`/`XHR`; `serviceWorkers:'block'` mandatory; the manifest's non-redirecting declaration required (rev 13 7g.2 rule 4); **and the origin-equality gate of 8a.1 passed before arming**. A navigation-triggered protected action is refused before arming (`POLICY_PROTECTED_NAVIGATION_UNSUPPORTED`, exit 18); a non-same-origin protected action is refused before arming (`POLICY_PROTECTED_CROSS_ORIGIN_UNSUPPORTED`, exit 18).
- *Gains:* exactly one server transmission in the 4 same-origin POST cells measured; no landing hop; no cross-origin routed protected request is possible at all; residual-difference rows 1, 2 and 4 of 8a.3 do not arise.
- *Costs:* depends on the 8a.2 restatement of 6j.1(7); rows 3 and 5 to 9 of 8a.3 remain; the page sees 204; cross-origin protected actions are simply unavailable, which removes a large part of the feature's reach for multi-origin applications; 302, navigation, multipart, form submit, header fidelity, H-7 and PUT/PATCH/DELETE remain uncovered.
- *Preconditions:* the SPIKE-R extension of 8d (PUT/PATCH/DELETE through 301/302; same-origin cookie and header parity; HTTPS/HSTS rows); the `docs/pilot/limitations.md` text of 8a.2; the 18C verification case of 8a.5.

**Option B′ — Option A′ plus navigation-triggered protected actions, under all of five named conditions.** Presented as a conditional so the owner can judge, not as a recommendation: (i) the manifest declares the target route non-redirecting **and** the origin-equality gate of 8a.1 passes; (ii) sequential mode, a single page, no concurrent arming lease; (iii) Falcon observes the navigation response and **any** 3xx aborts the run as a post-send incident; (iv) the limitation text states plainly that for a navigation Falcon has **no** pre-send control and that a 307/308 body would already have reached the next hop; (v) the hop-0 re-check of 8a.1(5) is recorded as detection only.
- *Consequence:* buys navigation coverage at the cost of accepting an **unprevented** same-origin-declared-but-actually-redirecting transmission on a manifest violation — detection only. Because of 8a.1(4) and (6) the unprevented transmission can no longer be cross-origin at hop 0, but a browser-followed hop out of a manifest-violating route can still be, and Falcon only detects it. PRG form submits still look identical to the policed case until after the fact.

**Option C — no protected non-GET in Phase 18.** Protected non-GET actions are refused before arming for every hop class, every trigger and every origin; only read, assert and non-protected permitted actions run against authenticated pages; the loopback fixture path still demonstrates the approval capability (AC-051).
- *Consequence:* zero new residual from redirects, **no M-17 dependency at all**, no dependency on M1, no 6j.1(7) restatement, no SPIKE-R extension; the feature's destructive-action half ships in a later phase.

**The architecture does not choose. All three options are implementable as specified. The owner selects the scope; SEC-13 has already ruled on R-10 and no further Security ruling is a precondition to A′ or B′ beyond the verification of 8a.5.**

## 8b. m-46 — the capture check is a smoke check, stated honestly (REPLACES rev 13 7a.1(5) and the L18 "integrity" wording; AMENDS rev 13 7a.1(4), L20 and AC-095)

1. **Call form corrected.** Every introspection of a captured primitive uses `uncurry(Function.prototype.toString)(capture)`, never `Function.prototype.toString(capture)`. The rev 13 form ignores its argument and returns `function () { [native code] }` for a user arrow (SEC-13 T4, reproduced in 8.0 A3); as written it asserted nothing at all.
2. **Relabelled, with no integrity claim.** 7a.1(5) and case L18 are **smoke checks for misconfiguration and careless tampering**, not controls. The word "integrity" is removed from 7a.1(5), from L18 and from AC-095. The honest statement, which replaces the rev 13 assertion sentence, is:
   > The capture smoke check detects a capture that is not a function, a capture that was never uncurried, and a capture replaced by ordinary user code. It **cannot** distinguish a genuine uncurried capture from a behaviour-preserving bound wrapper planted before module load: both are bound functions, both stringify as `function () { [native code] }`, both have own property names exactly `["length","name"]`, and the only difference observed is `.name`, which is `configurable: true` on a bound function and is therefore attacker-settable (8.0 A3). **No integrity property is claimed.**
3. **7a.1(4) restated.** "Fail-closed at construction" is narrowed to: *a careless pre-load tamper — one that leaves a captured value non-callable — fails loudly at load with `PILOT_INTERNAL_ERROR` exit 18, `intrinsic_capture_failed`. A behaviour-preserving pre-load tamper does not fail at all: it passes the typeof assertion, the own-names check, both `toString` forms and the functional check, and a forged approval is then accepted (SEC-13 T5).* **The architecture does not claim that a pre-load tamper fails closed**, and the rev 13 sentence that could be read that way is WITHDRAWN and annotated at its site.
4. **L20 extended.** The documented-limitation case gains the **T5 variant**: a pre-load, behaviour-preserving `Function.prototype.bind` replacement (delivered by `node --require`) that leaves `WeakSet.prototype.add`/`has` genuine, obtains `uncurry`, returns bound wrappers for every capture, and is **accepted**. The case asserts that documented outcome and is marked in the suite as a documented limitation, never as a passing security control. *Inversion:* if either L20 variant ever reports a refusal, the pre-load scope statement of rev 13 7a.2 is stale and must be rewritten.
5. **7i flag 7 amended** to mention T5 explicitly, as SEC-13 asked.
6. **Nothing is weakened.** The post-load closure of m-39 (rev 13 7a, SEC-13 T1 to T3, CLOSED) is untouched. Only the claim attached to the smoke check changes.

## 8c. m-47 — fixture repurposing: four routes closed by assertion, the rest bounded under R-8 (REPLACES rev 13 7c.3's assertion list and 7c.4's coverage sentence; AMENDS AC-092)

### 8c.1 Additional assertions (at authority construction **and** at consume, both)

All comparisons use **uncurried captured** primitives taken at fixture-module load (rev 13 7a.1(2)), and all captured references are taken **before** the fixture module requires anything else.

1. `uncurry(Object.getPrototypeOf)(server) === CAPTURED_HTTP_SERVER_PROTOTYPE` — closes route (a) (8.0 A1).
2. `server.emit === CAPTURED_EE_EMIT` **and** `CAPTURED_EE_EMIT === EventEmitter.prototype.emit` — the second conjunct is what closes route (b), a post-load patch of the live prototype method; the first alone does not (8.0 A1).
3. `server._handle` is present, and **every** own symbol key of `server._handle` whose description matches the owner-symbol form references `server` itself — closes route (c). The check fails closed if `_handle` is absent or exposes no owner symbol, rather than passing vacuously.
4. The own `Symbol(IncomingMessage)` and `Symbol(ServerResponse)` properties of the server, when present, are **reference-identical** to the captured `http.IncomingMessage` and `http.ServerResponse` — closes route (d). Absence is not a pass: if either symbol is absent on a Node version where the baseline recorded it, that is a failure.

Any failure is `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` **exit 14**, reason `fixture_server_repurposed`, as for the rev 13 checks.

### 8c.2 Listener accounting replaced by a whole-snapshot equality (closes the `setTimeout` gap)

Rev 13's five named events (`request`, `connection`, `upgrade`, `checkContinue`, `clientError`) are **replaced** by a complete snapshot: at the `listening` event the record stores `eventNames(server)` with the listener count of each name, read through uncurried captured `EventEmitter.prototype.eventNames` and `listenerCount`. At construction and at consume, the live snapshot must be **exactly equal** — same name set, same counts, same order-independent comparison. Baseline on Node v22.19.0: **`request 1, connection 1, listening 1`** (8.0 A2).

`server.setTimeout(ms, fn)` adds a `timeout` listener (`0 → 1`, 8.0 A2) and is therefore now detected, where rev 13's fixed five-event list missed it entirely. The fixture app does not call `setTimeout` on the server; if it ever needs to, the call must happen **before** the `listening` event so that it is part of the recorded baseline, and that ordering is a normative constraint on the fixture module.

### 8c.3 What is **not** closed — stated residual under R-8

1. **Any pre-load tamper.** A patch of `EventEmitter.prototype.emit`, `http.Server.prototype`, `net.Server.prototype.listen` or the `http` classes applied **before** the fixture module takes its captures is invisible to every comparison in 8c.1, because the captured and live references then agree. This is the same class as rev 13 7a.2 (pre-load execution; routes `--require`, `NODE_OPTIONS`, repository modification, modified binary or `node_modules`) and sits inside OWNER-10 and residuals 13 and 24. SEC-13's alternative — "record (b) under R-8 explicitly" — is adopted **for the pre-load half only**; the post-load half of (b) is closed by assertion 8c.1(2).
2. **Point-in-time checking (TOCTOU).** The assertions run at construction and at consume. A tamper applied **after** the consume check and before the request is served is not detected. No claim is made that the fixture server is continuously verified.
3. **A `FIXTURE_HANDLER` whose own body was edited in the fixture module source.** Identity is preserved by construction; this is repository trust, OWNER-10, already under R-8 (rev 13 7c.4). Unchanged.
4. **Socket-level interception from Node's own `connection` listener, and the `clientError` path.** Detected only to the extent that it changes the listener snapshot or an own property; a handler that intercepts at the socket without changing either is not detected. Stated, not closed.
5. **A substituted `_handle` that is a compatible object preserving the owner symbol pointing at `server`.** Assertion 8c.1(3) checks the owner linkage, not the authenticity of the handle object itself. Stated, not closed.

**The 7c.4 coverage sentence is corrected** to read, in effect: *these checks refuse listener replacement, `prependListener` forwarding, an own `emit` shadow, an extra listener on any event including `timeout`, a prototype swap, a post-load `emit` prototype patch, an `_handle` owner swap and a class-symbol swap. They do not refuse any pre-load tamper, a post-consume tamper, an edited handler body, socket-level interception that preserves the snapshot, or a compatible substituted `_handle`. Those five are stated residuals under R-8 and are not accepted here.*

## 8d. m-48 — SPIKE-R coverage gaps become stated extension conditions (AMENDS rev 13 7g.3's non-coverage list, 7g.4 Option A's gains, and the SPIKE-R `openItems` text)

1. **PUT, PATCH and DELETE were never measured.** Both SPIKE-R parts used GET and POST only, although rev 12 6j.2 required all four. Under the Fetch standard, 301, 302 and 303 rewrite **only** POST, so a PUT, PATCH or DELETE through 301 or 302 would keep its method **and its body**. Footnote 1's "did not produce this hop class" therefore holds **for POST only**, and the cell is probably real for the other methods: **UNVERIFIED**.
2. **The cross-origin Q3 cells do not discriminate.** All 8 cross-origin baselines were `Failed to fetch` with no landing hop, so the sanitized cross-origin cells do not show that M1 prevents a cross-origin body leak. The Option A gain "no cross-origin body leak in those cells" is **struck** from 7g.4 (and does not reappear in Option A′, 8a.4, whose cross-origin claim rests on the 8a.1 refusal rather than on measurement).
3. **Merge conditions.** Both gaps are added to the 7g.3 non-coverage list (rows 1 and 9 of 8a.3) and to the SPIKE-R `openItems` text, and both are **merge conditions for 18B and 18C under any option other than C**. Required rows before any such merge:
   - same-origin **PUT, PATCH and DELETE** through 301, 302, 303, 307 and 308, with server-side ground truth for method and body at every hop;
   - a **same-origin cookie and header parity** row: the browser's own request versus the `route.fetch` request, field by field, including the absence of `Sec-Fetch-*`;
   - **HTTPS and HSTS** rows (and, if cheap, mixed content and Private Network Access), covering row 5 of 8a.3;
   - repetition of T7 and of the sanitized same-origin cells (SEC-13 records T7 as a single run).
4. **Footnote correction.** Footnote 1 of the 7g.1 table is amended to say "for POST only; PUT, PATCH and DELETE were not measured (UNVERIFIED)". The UNOBSERVABLE¹ label stays correct only for non-GET, as SEC-13 states.
5. **Option C carries none of these conditions**, which is the cheapest path to a Phase 18 merge and is one reason the owner may prefer it. This is a consequence, not a recommendation.
